# AI in Dutch business: use rises with size, readiness lags behind

*Prepared for the ros.ai demo from the findings and calculations on this canvas. Each source and figure links to the saved page or result it comes from.*

## The bigger the firm, the more likely it uses AI

In 2024, nearly 23% of Dutch companies with ten or more workers used at least one AI technology, nearly 9 points more than a year earlier ([CBS](artifact:{{page:cbs-news}})). Use climbs with every size class in [CBS's table](artifact:{{dataset}}): from 17.8% of firms with 10 to 19 workers to 59.2% of firms with 500 or more, a gap of [41.4 percentage points](artifact:{{metric:gap}}).

![AI adoption by company size · CBS 2024](artifact:{{plot:adoption}})

The pattern is not only Dutch. Across the EU in 2025, 17% of small enterprises, 30.36% of medium ones and 55.03% of large ones used AI ([Eurostat](artifact:{{page:eurostat}})).

## How you ask changes the number

CBS counts firms that use at least one of seven specific technologies, such as machine learning and text mining ([CBS](artifact:{{page:cbs-summary}})). By a similar count, almost 14% of micro businesses, with two to ten people, used AI in 2025 ([MKB Servicedesk](artifact:{{page:micro}})).

Surveys that ask whether a firm uses AI at all find far more: 70% of entrepreneurs in Exact's MKB Barometer, up from 33% in 2025 ([Dutch IT Channel](artifact:{{page:exact}})), and 78% of organisations with turnover up to €25 million in ABN AMRO's own research ([ABN AMRO Insights](artifact:{{page:abn-risk}})). An article has to say which measure it uses.

## Turnover shares are not AI revenue

CBS also reports how much of each size class's turnover belongs to firms that use AI ([CBS](artifact:{{page:cbs-chapter}})). Among the largest firms, AI users hold 79.5% of turnover while making up 59.2% of firms: 20.3 points more. In two middle classes, 20 to 49 and 50 to 99 workers, AI users hold slightly less turnover than their share of firms.

![Where AI users hold more turnover than their numbers](artifact:{{plot:gap}})

Turnover that belongs to AI users is not turnover caused by AI. CBS itself explains the link with company size partly by larger companies being more likely to use AI.

![Challenge the denominator · adoption versus turnover](artifact:{{plot:denominator}})

## Use runs ahead of readiness

Only 9% of firms with turnover up to €25 million have a formal policy for outside AI tools, and smaller firms in particular are not yet prepared for a new generation of digital risks ([ABN AMRO Insights](artifact:{{page:abn-risk}})). Among firms that considered AI but did not use it, lack of experience was by far the most common reason, at 75% ([CBS](artifact:{{page:cbs-summary}})).

The payoff follows size too. A BIS study of European firms finds that AI adoption raises labour productivity by 4%, with the gains concentrated in medium and large firms ([BIS](artifact:{{page:bis}})).

## What a bank could ask its business clients

- Which AI tools does the business use, and for what?
- Is there a policy for outside AI tools, and who checks it?
- Does the team have the experience to use AI well, or does it need help?
- For smaller clients: what would make AI worth the effort?

## What the evidence does not show

- The CBS figures cover firms with ten or more workers in the sectors CBS surveys (C to N and Q), and the 2024 figures are provisional. Micro businesses are counted in a separate report.
- These sources measure whether firms use AI, not what AI does to a firm's results.
- CBS and the surveys ask different questions, so their numbers cannot be compared one to one.
- The figures describe groups of firms, not any single client.
